The Relational Shape of Structural Complexity and Neighborhood Density

More detail does not merely make an object longer to describe. When the new detail distinguishes it from its peers, the object moves outward in relational space and its nearby alternatives can only disappear.

The result in one sentence

Under an additive distance with nonnegative coordinate contributions, adding distinguishing structure can never create a new fixed-radius neighbor.

The argument

Description becomes geometry in one line

The paper starts with a finite population of objects. Each added coordinate is allowed to separate a pair or leave it unchanged, but never to contribute negative distance.

01 · Describe

Objects acquire coordinates

At complexity $N$, object $O_i$ is represented by $O_i^{(N)}=(o_{i1},\ldots,o_{iN})$. The coordinates may be categorical, numerical, or any features with a nonnegative dissimilarity.

02 · Separate

Differences accumulate

The next coordinate adds $d_{N+1}\ge0$ to every pairwise distance. A shared or redundant coordinate adds nothing; a genuinely distinguishing one pushes at least some pairs apart.

03 · Thin

Nearby alternatives disappear

Because no distance decreases, an object already outside a fixed-radius neighborhood cannot re-enter merely because more structure was appended.

An ordinary description tells us what an object contains. A relational description also tells us which other objects could still plausibly be mistaken for it. That second view is the paper’s central shift: complexity is visible not only inside the object, but in the changing population around it.

Imagine adding one field at a time to a scientific catalog. A field shared by every entry changes the file format but not the geometry of the catalog. A field that splits the population into distinct values does something more consequential: some previously nearby entries are now farther apart. The theorem isolates exactly this second kind of structure.

Key formalism

The theorem and its filtration

The result is deterministic. It requires neither randomness nor a physical interpretation.

Additive separation

$$ \delta_N(O_i,O_j)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_n(o_{in},o_{jn}), \qquad d_n(x,y)\ge0. $$ $$ \delta_{N+1}=\delta_N+d_{N+1}\ge\delta_N. $$

The assumption is cumulative, unnormalized distance. If the distance itself is renormalized when $N$ changes, the conclusion need not follow.

Neighborhood density

$$ \rho_N(i;r)=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j\ne i} \mathbf1\!\left\{\delta_N(O_i,O_j)\le r\right\}. $$ $$ \rho_{N+1}(i;r)\le\rho_N(i;r). $$

Each indicator can switch from one to zero, or remain fixed. None can switch from zero back to one.

Nested relational neighborhoods

$$ \mathcal N_r^{(1)}(O_i)\supseteq \mathcal N_r^{(2)}(O_i)\supseteq\cdots\supseteq \mathcal N_r^{(N)}(O_i)\supseteq\cdots $$

This descending filtration is the mathematical object carried into the next three papers. Later papers replace abstract objects by histories and cardinality by invariant physical measure.

The filtration is stronger than a statement about an average trend. It follows every individual neighbor. Once a particular alternative has crossed beyond radius $r$, later nonnegative refinements cannot bring it back. This pathwise monotonicity is why the construction can later be reinterpreted as survival through accumulated constraints.

What a coordinate contributes

Coordinate count is not the same as distinguishing information

A useful coordinate is one that changes relations within the population. The paper makes that statement quantitative.

Isolation velocity

$$ v_i(N)=D_{N+1}(i)-D_N(i) =\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j\ne i}d_{N+1}(o_{i,N+1},o_{j,N+1})\ge0. $$

A population-wide constant coordinate has $v_i=0$. A strongly differentiating coordinate produces a larger outward step.

Categorical information

For Hamming distance on a categorical coordinate with probabilities $p_n(x)$:

$$ \Pr(o_{in}\ne o_{jn})=1-\sum_x p_n(x)^2. $$

The collision term is related to Rényi entropy, $H_2=-\log\sum_xp_n(x)^2$. Diversity, not metadata length alone, controls expected separation.

Exact

Nested

Every fixed-radius neighborhood is a subset of the one before it.

Uniform q-ary model

$\Theta(N^R q^{-N})$

At fixed radius $R$, expected local density decays exponentially up to a polynomial factor.

Interpretation

Relational

An identity is expressed partly through the alternatives that cease to remain nearby.

The stochastic model is illustrative rather than required. It shows how a deterministic ordering principle can acquire a quantitative rate once an ensemble is specified. The exponential thinning law is therefore not a universal law of complexity; it is the consequence of a particular independent metadata model layered on top of the exact filtration.

Scope of the result

What is proved—and what has not entered yet

This paper is intentionally prior to dynamics and cosmology.

The theorem does not identify a physical distance

  • Appending metadata is not enough; the coordinate must contribute nonnegative pairwise dissimilarity.
  • Redundant structure may increase description length without changing any neighborhood.
  • No law of motion or cosmological scale factor follows from neighborhood thinning alone.
  • The next paper adds the conjectural step: let accumulated history supply the distinguishing coordinates.

The handoff to Paper 2

What if the added structure is the past?

A longer history can distinguish present states that once appeared equivalent. Paper 2 asks whether the resulting loss of compatible alternatives can be read as an expanding relational volume.

$$ \text{more history}\longrightarrow \text{fewer compatible alternatives}\longrightarrow \text{a possible geometric scale?} $$
