A research program in mathematical physics

The present may be defined as much by the histories it can no longer have as by the state it is in.

Compatible Histories follows one idea through four papers: added information separates alternatives; a longer past makes that separation physical; dynamics decides which histories are admissible; and invariant measure tells us how much possibility remains.

The idea in one sentence

As a world accumulates history, alternatives that once looked compatible fall away; the program asks whether the invariant rate of that loss has a geometric expression in cosmic expansion.

The narrative arc

Four papers, each replacing a loose idea with a sharper object

The sequence begins with a clean combinatorial fact and ends with a concrete classical history measure. At each turn, a tempting shortcut is tested—and either refined or ruled out.

More distinction means fewer neighbors

Append a nonnegative distinguishing coordinate and no pair can become closer. Every fixed-radius neighborhood can only stay the same or shrink.

Theorem: complexity has a relational shape.

Let history do the distinguishing

A longer shared past can expose differences between candidate worlds. Their survival information suggests a relational volume—and a provocative bridge to expansion.

Hypothesis: lost alternatives may acquire geometric scale.

Replace proximity with physics

Arbitrary tolerances and raw cell counts fail invariance tests. Noether constraints define admissibility; Liouville phase volume supplies coordinate-independent weight.

Correction: count physical possibility, not grid cells.

Measure complete histories

Deterministic dynamics maps reduced initial data to entire trajectories. Pushing invariant phase measure through that map avoids counting every time slice as independent.

Construction: measure solution space, not a stack of snapshots.

The mathematical spine

From thinning neighborhoods to invariant history information

A small chain of definitions carries the argument. The arrows are mathematical; the final identification with cosmic expansion remains a physical hypothesis.

Distinguishing structure accumulates

At descriptive depth $N$, separation is additive and nonnegative:

$$ \delta_N(O_i,O_j)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_n(o_{in},o_{jn}), \qquad d_n\ge 0. $$

Therefore $\delta_{N+1}\ge\delta_N$ and fixed-radius neighborhoods form a descending filtration, $\mathcal N_r^{(N+1)}(O_i)\subseteq\mathcal N_r^{(N)}(O_i)$.

Survival becomes information

If $\rho(t)$ is the fraction of alternatives still compatible with a shared past, define

$$ C(t)=-\log_2\rho(t). $$

As compatible alternatives disappear, $C$ grows. The logarithm turns multiplicative survival fractions into additive bits.

Dynamics supplies the measure

Physical initial data $z\in\Gamma_{\rm phys}$ determine complete histories through the solution map $F$:

$$ F:\Gamma_{\rm phys}\to\mathcal H, \qquad \mu_{\mathcal H}=F_*\nu_0. $$

The history measure is induced from reduced Liouville measure; it is not an independent product over arbitrarily many temporal slices.

Compatibility weights whole trajectories

For a history functional $0\le W_t(\gamma)\le1$:

$$ Z_t=\int_{\mathcal H}W_t(\gamma)\,d\mu_{\mathcal H}(\gamma), \qquad C_{\rm surv}(t)=-\log_2 Z_t. $$

The lapse removes clock-density artifacts; the gauge-fixed determinant removes orbit-counting artifacts.

The cosmological bridge under investigation

The proposed dictionary turns inverse surviving-history measure into a three-dimensional relational scale:

$$ \rho\longrightarrow C=-\log_2\rho \longrightarrow a_{\rm rel}\propto 2^{C/3} \longrightarrow H_{\rm rel}=\frac{\ln 2}{3}\frac{dC}{d\tau}. $$

Boundary of the result: the program now has an invariant finite classical candidate for $C$. It has not derived the last relation from gravity or shown that $H_{\rm rel}$ equals the observed Hubble rate.

What the evidence says

A program built to keep theorem, test, failure, and conjecture separate

The most interesting result is not a single favorable number. It is the progressive narrowing of what the physical object can be.

Proved

Neighborhood thinning

With additive nonnegative coordinate contributions, increasing distinguishing complexity produces nested, nonincreasing fixed-radius neighborhoods.

Validated in finite systems

Invariant classical measure

Conditional estimators remove predictable dynamics, while reduced Liouville measure survives tested nonlinear canonical transformations, clock changes, and gauge fixing.

Ruled out

A fixed information rate

The simple laws $a(t)\propto t$ and $a(t)\propto t^{4/3}$ do not fit the DESI DR2 BAO expansion history. Causal-past four-volume is also inadequate as a fixed-coupling proxy.

Open

The gravitational connection

Whether a covariant, gauge-invariant loss rate on gravitational solution space tracks cosmological expansion has not been derived or established.

The next scientific gate

Move the invariant object into gravity—before comparing another rate

The next test is a finite gravitational or cosmological constrained system: construct its reduced symplectic measure, define a causal relational compatibility functional, and require the result to survive canonical, clock, gauge, and refinement changes.

$$ \text{gravitational solution space} \longrightarrow \text{invariant surviving measure} \longrightarrow \frac{dC_{\rm surv}}{d\tau} \stackrel{?}{\longleftrightarrow} H(\tau). $$

Only after the measure and normalization are fixed independently should its rate be compared with held-out expansion data. Quantum histories remain a distinct later extension.
