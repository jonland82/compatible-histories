Hubble Scale from Time-Induced Complexity

If a present state must remain consistent with an ever longer past, then time continuously removes alternative histories. This paper asks whether the rate of that loss can set a cosmic expansion scale—and lets cosmological data reject the simplest version.

The productive clue

The age of the Universe naturally produces the present Hubble scale, but the full expansion history shows that compatible-history information cannot accumulate at a fixed rate per doubling of time.

From Paper 1 to cosmology

Let the past supply the distinguishing coordinates

Paper 1 proved that added distinctions thin fixed-radius neighborhoods. Here the objects are candidate histories with a shared resolved past, and later times expose distinctions that earlier slices could not.

01 · Survive

Track compatible alternatives

Let $\rho(t)$ be the fraction of candidate histories still compatible at time $t$. The history filtration implies $\rho$ cannot increase as independent constraints accumulate.

02 · Inform

Turn survival into bits

Define $C=-\log_2\rho$. If one history in $2^C$ remains compatible, then $C$ bits distinguish it from the original ensemble.

03 · Hypothesize

Give inverse density a scale

Treat $\rho^{-1}$ as a relational volume, take its cube root as a three-dimensional scale, and ask whether that scale is the cosmological scale factor.

The proposed dictionary

$$ \rho\longrightarrow C=-\log_2\rho \longrightarrow V_{\rm rel}\propto2^C \longrightarrow a_{\rm rel}\propto2^{C/3} \longrightarrow H_{\rm rel}=\frac{\ln2}{3}\dot C. $$

Logical boundary: thinning is inherited from Paper 1. Identifying inverse history density with volume—and $a_{\rm rel}$ with the physical FLRW scale factor—is an added cosmological hypothesis.

The simple model

A constant number of distinctions per doubling of age

The cleanest scale-free assumption makes an immediate prediction. It is striking at the present epoch and too rigid across cosmic history.

Fixed information rate

$$ C(t)-C_*=d_{\rm eff}\log_2(t/t_*). $$ $$ a(t)\propto t^p,\qquad H(t)=\frac{p}{t},\qquad p=\frac{d_{\rm eff}}{3}. $$

Three spatial channels give $d_{\rm eff}=3$, $a\propto t$, and $H=1/t$. Counting time as a fourth interchangeable coordinate gives $a\propto t^{4/3}$.

The present scale

$$ t_0=13.80\,\mathrm{Gyr} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_0^{(3)}=\frac1{t_0} =70.85\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}. $$

This is a few-percent match to current determinations. But $H_0t_0\approx1$ in the observed Universe, and the age is model-inferred, so this is a scale consistency—not an independent measurement.

The decisive test

One correct scale does not make a correct expansion history

DESI DR2 BAO distances test the redshift-dependent shape after profiling out the overall distance scale. The constant-rate models fail clearly.

Dynamic information rate

$$ d_{\rm eff}(t)=\frac{dC}{d\log_2t}=3H(t)t. $$

Expansion data can be read as a target curve for history information. The fixed models assume this curve is constant.

Power-law observable

$$ H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{1/p}, \qquad D_H=\frac cH, \qquad D_M=c\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}. $$

These equations give the full BAO distance–redshift shape for a constant $p$.

What the failure teaches

No constant number of effective bits per logarithmic time captures all epochs. Even fitting the power law below $z=1$ yields $\chi^2=670.72$ on six held-out predictions at $z\ge1$. The idea must become dynamical—or replace total accumulation with conditional new information.

The relativistic correction

Count what is new, not the common past again

Total causal-past four-volume is covariant in spirit but repeats shared history at every step. Tests show that it is not the missing information variable.

Required refinement

Conditional

Scientific status

The present-day scale connection is a clue. The fixed cosmology is ruled out. No independent covariant $C(t)$ has yet been constructed, and the relation $H=(\ln2/3)\dot C$ remains hypothesized.
